Legionella risk assessments in London

Converted Victorian houses, HMOs, mansion blocks, offices and schools, all on very hard water. We're based in Chiswick and we're out across the capital every week.

Call 020 3633 6064

London is where we do more Legionella risk assessments than anywhere else. We work out of Chiswick, W4, so the capital is on our doorstep, and the city's rented housing throws up the same handful of water problems again and again. Here's what we tend to find, and what it means if you let or manage property here.

London's rented homes and their plumbing

A huge share of Londoners rent, and a lot of those homes weren't built as flats. Victorian and Edwardian terraces in places like Hackney, Lambeth, Islington and Ealing have been split into two, three or four units, usually by different builders at different times. The result is plumbing that's been rerouted, extended and capped off over decades. Capped-off branches that still hold water (dead legs, in the trade) are one of the most common findings in a London conversion.

Some of these buildings still have a shared cold water tank in the loft feeding several flats, sometimes with a lid that's a sheet of hardboard. Others have a mix of combi boilers in some flats and an old vented cylinder in another. Each set-up carries a different level of risk, and a decent assessment should spell out who's responsible for which bit. That matters when the freeholder, the managing agent and three leaseholders all assume someone else is looking after the tank.

Mansion blocks and purpose-built flats

Red-brick mansion blocks around Maida Vale, Battersea, Marylebone and Kensington often have communal hot water or large central storage in a basement plant room. Long pipe runs, risers that get warm and flats that sit empty while they're between lets or being refurbished all make temperature control harder. Post-war council estates and newer blocks of flats bring their own versions of the same issue, with booster pumps, roof tanks and little-used cleaners' sinks and bin-store taps.

HMOs

London has a lot of houses in multiple occupation, and many boroughs run additional or selective licensing schemes on top of the national mandatory scheme. Check your own council's rules, because conditions and paperwork vary from borough to borough. Even where a Legionella risk assessment isn't specifically named, having a current one makes the water side of a licence application or inspection much simpler. Our guide to HMO licensing and Legionella goes through what councils commonly look for.

Very hard water, and why it matters

Most of London is supplied by Thames Water. Affinity Water serves parts of the north and north-west, parts of the east are on Essex & Suffolk Water and parts of the far south are on SES Water. Almost all of it is hard to very hard, because so much of it comes from the chalk and the Thames and Lee. You can check your own street with the Thames Water postcode checker, or your supplier's equivalent if you're not on Thames.

Hard water leaves limescale, and scale gives bacteria somewhere to settle. In London flats we routinely find shower heads furred up inside, calcified hoses and TMV strainers clogged with grit. That's why descaling showers is a standard part of Legionella maintenance here, and why TMV servicing intervals in London can end up shorter than you'd expect.

FAQs

London questions

Do London landlords need a Legionella risk assessment?

Yes. Landlords anywhere in England, London included, have a legal duty to assess and control the Legionella risk in the homes they let. There's no set format or certificate, but a written assessment is the simplest way to show you've done it. In London it's also worth having one ready for HMO licensing, because many boroughs run additional or selective schemes with their own conditions.

Does London's hard water increase the Legionella risk?

It can make control harder. Hard water leaves limescale inside shower heads, hoses, taps and valves, and that scale gives bacteria a sheltered place to settle. It doesn't cause Legionella on its own, but in London it's a good reason to descale showers regularly and to keep an eye on TMV strainers, which clog up faster here than in soft water areas.

Who is responsible for a shared loft tank in a converted house?

Usually whoever controls the common parts, often the freeholder or a residents' management company, but it depends on the lease. Many London conversions have one cold water tank feeding several flats, and it's easy for everyone to assume someone else is checking it. A risk assessment should name who's responsible for each part of the system so nothing falls between the gaps.

Can you visit flats in controlled parking zones?

Yes, we work in CPZs across London all the time. A visitor permit or a suspended bay helps a lot, so let us know what's available when you book. The ULEZ and the central Congestion Charge are on us to plan for, not you. We can also arrange access directly with tenants if that's easier for you.
