TMV servicing & testing

Thermostatic mixing valves let you store water hot enough to kill Legionella and still deliver it safe enough not to scald. But scale and debris build up inside them, and a neglected valve can fail hot or fail lukewarm. We keep yours working.

Call 020 3633 6064
Why it matters

A failed TMV is a risk both ways

If a valve fails hot, it can cause serious scalds, especially to children, older people and anyone with reduced mobility or sensation.

If it drifts too cool or the cold supply takes over, the water downstream sits at the temperature Legionella likes best, between 20°C and 45°C.

Regular servicing catches both problems before they reach a resident, pupil or patient.

What's included

Every TMV service covers

Each valve is tested, serviced and recorded, so you've got evidence for your logbook, your insurer and your inspector.

Temperature check

Mixed water temperature measured at the outlet, and compared against the correct setting for that outlet.

Fail-safe test

We isolate the cold supply to prove the valve shuts off safely instead of delivering scalding water.

Strip-down & descale

Valve opened up, cartridge, strainers and check valves cleaned and descaled, then inspected for wear.

Parts replaced

Worn cartridges, seals, strainers and check valves replaced. We'll always tell you before fitting anything major.

Re-commissioning

Valve reset to the right temperature for its use and retested, so it leaves working exactly as it should.

Asset register & certificate

Every valve tagged and logged with its location, type, readings and next service date.

TMV2 or TMV3?

We service every common type

TMV2 valves

For domestic-type settings: homes, HMOs, hotels and some schools. Approved to BS EN 1111 and BS EN 1287.

TMV3 valves

For healthcare and care settings, approved to NHS Model Engineering Specification D08 for higher performance and fail-safe requirements.

Point-of-use & group valves

Under-basin valves serving a single tap, and larger valves supplying a bank of showers or washbasins.

FAQs

TMV servicing questions

How often should TMVs be serviced?

It depends on the site and how the valves performed last time. Most sites service at least once a year. Healthcare and care settings often test more often, especially after installation or a failed test. We'll recommend an interval for each valve based on its results.

How long does it take?

Usually well under an hour per valve, depending on access and condition. We'll plan the visit so bathrooms are out of use for as little time as possible.

Can you install new TMVs?

Yes. We fit new valves where your risk assessment calls for scald protection, and replace valves that are beyond economic repair. See remedial works.

We don't know how many TMVs we have.

That's very common. On the first visit we'll find and tag every valve and build an asset register, so future servicing is quick and nothing gets missed.

What's the difference between TMV2 and TMV3?

TMV2 and TMV3 are certification schemes for thermostatic mixing valves. TMV2 covers valves for domestic-type use, tested to BS EN 1111 and BS EN 1287. TMV3 is the healthcare scheme, based on the NHS performance specification D 08, and is the usual choice in care homes, hospitals and anywhere vulnerable people bathe. We service both, and we'll tell you if a valve isn't right for where it's fitted.

What temperature should a TMV be set to?

It depends on the outlet and who uses it. HSE's guidance for health and social care says water for whole-body bathing shouldn't exceed 44°C where vulnerable people are at risk, and NHS guidance gives lower maximums for showers and washbasins, typically 41°C. Your scalding risk assessment should set the temperature for each outlet, and we check every valve against it when we service.
